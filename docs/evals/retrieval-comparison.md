Dataset: `evals/datasets/retrieval_v1.json` v1.0.0 — 20 golden queries (6 lexical, 9 semantic, 5 mixed).

Generated by `python -m evals.run retrieval`. Every run uses the demo embedding provider so the numbers are reproducible on any machine, including CI with no credentials.

## Decision: `hybrid`

Selected on primary-in-top-5 (20/20) then MRR (0.887) — the two things a traveller feels: did the right source appear at all, and how far down. `—` in the table above means the primary source was not retrieved in the top 8.

`keyword` and `hybrid` are **tied on NDCG@8** (0.8134 vs 0.8130) — a gap far inside the noise of a 20-case set. The tie is broken on MRR, which is a real difference, not on the fourth decimal of NDCG.

Read the per-class table before generalising from the headline: the retrievers fail on different queries, which is the argument for fusing them rather than picking one.

### Does reranking earn its place?

| | NDCG@8 | MRR | Primary in top-5 | p50 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hybrid` | 0.813 | 0.887 | 20/20 | 24ms |
| `hybrid_rerank` | 0.811 | 0.873 | 19/20 | 24ms |

**No, not on this corpus.** Reranking moves NDCG@8 by -0.002 and primary-in-top-5 by -1, in exchange for extra latency and, in its LLM form, a model call. It stays implemented and configurable (`RETRIEVAL_STRATEGY=hybrid_rerank`) but is **not** the default. Reranking earns its place by adding precision over a large candidate set; 20 queries over a few dozen chunks is not that. Re-evaluate as the corpus grows.

### Caveat on corpus size

These numbers come from a small, hand-authored corpus whose vocabulary overlaps the golden queries more than a scraped corpus would, which flatters lexical search. The ranking of strategies should be re-measured after switching to a real embedding provider and as the corpus grows — both are called out in docs/deployment.md.

Reproduce with:

```bash
python -m evals.run retrieval      # the four-way comparison
python -m evals.runners.sweep      # rrf_k, dense weight, reranker weights
```
